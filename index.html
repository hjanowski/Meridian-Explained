How Meridian Works

Meridian is Google's open-source Marketing Mix Model. It uses Bayesian statistics to understand the true relationship between your marketing activity and business outcomes — so you can optimise every pound of your budget.

The Core Question
How does marketing activity
drive business results?
On one side you have activity data from each media channel — TV, Radio, Social, Search, Display — measured by impressions, clicks, and spend. On the other side you have business KPIs — revenue and conversions. But revenue doesn't just depend on marketing. External factors like weather, seasonal festivities, the economy, and promotions also drive sales. Meridian's job is to untangle all of these forces and figure out the true causal link between your marketing and your outcomes.

Under the Hood
Four steps to understanding
Meridian doesn't just correlate spend with revenue. It models the mechanics of how marketing works — including delayed effects and diminishing returns — to separate genuine impact from coincidence.
01

Collect Historical Data

Meridian ingests weekly (or daily) time-series data for every media channel — TV, Radio, Social, Search, Display — including spend, impressions, and clicks. Crucially, it also takes in external control variables: weather patterns, seasonal festivities (Christmas, Black Friday), economic indicators, and promotions. These controls let the model separate "revenue went up because of our TV campaign" from "revenue went up because it was Christmas."

02

Transform with Adstock & Saturation

Raw spend doesn't map directly to impact. Meridian applies adstock (a TV ad seen today still influences purchases next week) and saturation curves (the 10th million spent on a channel yields less than the 1st). These transforms reflect how marketing actually behaves in the real world.

03

Fit a Bayesian Regression

Meridian builds a regression: KPI = f(TV, Radio, Social, Search, … , weather, seasonality, promotions). By including external factors alongside media channels, the model can isolate the incremental effect of each channel — the uplift your marketing creates above and beyond what would have happened anyway. Being Bayesian, it gives you uncertainty estimates and confidence intervals, not just point estimates.

04

Attribute & Optimise

The fitted model tells you how much each channel contributed to your KPI. You can then run budget optimisation: given a fixed budget, Meridian recommends the allocation across channels that maximises expected revenue or conversions.

Key Concepts
Adstock & Saturation
These two transformations are what make Meridian's understanding realistic. Without them, the model would assume that spend instantly and linearly translates to outcomes — which isn't how marketing works.

Adstock (Carryover Effect)

When you run a TV campaign this week, its effect doesn't vanish overnight. People remember the ad, and purchases may happen days or weeks later. Meridian models this decay — a fraction of this week's spend "carries over" into the next, and the next, gradually fading. This means a spike in spend creates a smoothed-out, lingering impact on your KPI.

Saturation (Diminishing Returns)

The first £1M you spend on search ads reaches eager buyers. The 10th £1M reaches people who were never going to convert. Meridian uses a Hill function to model this curve: impact rises steeply at first, then flattens. This is critical for budget optimisation — it tells you when a channel has "enough" spend.
The Engine
Bayesian Inference
Meridian doesn't produce a single "answer." It uses Bayesian statistics to explore thousands of plausible models, giving you a distribution of outcomes — and honest uncertainty about what it knows and doesn't know.
𝒫

Prior Beliefs

Start with sensible assumptions — e.g., "spend on TV probably has a positive effect on revenue." These can encode expert knowledge or research findings like Google's reach and frequency data.

↓ combine with data ↓
𝒟

Observed Data (Likelihood)

Your historical marketing data — all the spend, impressions, and outcomes — is used to update those initial beliefs. Where data is strong, it overrides the prior; where data is weak, the prior provides stability.

↓ produces ↓
✓

Posterior Distribution

The result: a full probability distribution for each channel's effect. Instead of "TV ROI = 2.3x", you get "TV ROI is most likely between 1.8x and 2.9x, with 90% confidence." This honesty is crucial for real decision-making.

What You Get
From Understanding to Action
Once Meridian understands the relationship between your activity data and KPIs, it produces three actionable outputs.
📊

Channel Attribution

How much of your total revenue or conversions was driven by each marketing channel — with confidence intervals so you know which estimates to trust.

💡

ROI Estimates

For every pound spent on each channel, how much incremental revenue did it generate? Meridian gives you a distribution, not a point estimate, so you can make risk-aware decisions.

🎯

Budget Optimisation

Given your total budget, Meridian recommends the optimal split across channels that maximises your chosen KPI — whether that's revenue, conversions, or another metric.

The key insight: Meridian doesn't just find correlations. By modelling carryover effects, diminishing returns, accounting for external factors like weather and seasonal demand, and using Bayesian priors, it builds a causal understanding of how your marketing activity translates into business results — separating genuine marketing impact from background noise — and uses that understanding to make your budget work harder.
